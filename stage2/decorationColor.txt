 Figma's approach is more disciplined than it first looks. They don't use 4 accents interchangeably — each color has a fixed home and only appears there. That keeps the canvas calm even though the palette is wider.

Here's how I'd adapt that for StyleBI:

The discipline that makes it work
Two layers:

Action layer — orange. Universal, always means "do this." Never replaced.
Identity layer — per-destination accent. Each top-level area gets one. Appears only in that area's decorative imagery.
Where the identity color may appear:

The destination's icon in the rail (active state)
Empty-state illustrations / thumbnail accents (template stripes, pattern-tile mark)
Maybe a thin top-edge stripe on section headers within that area
Where it never appears:

Home (Home is the system view — orange-only)
Body chrome, cards, borders, hover states across destinations (those stay neutral)
Any CTA button (CTAs are always orange)
Any text run (color is for marks, not type)

The Figma test
In Figma you can scan a page and tell which area you're in by glancing at one mark — usually the rail icon. The chrome stays gray. We'd want the same: someone screenshotting our Dashboards page sees one indigo mark; on Data, one teal mark. Otherwise neutral.
